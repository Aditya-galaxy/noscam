How it works

  1. 01

    It notices how you got there

    Typed it yourself, opened a bookmark, or clicked a link in WhatsApp, Telegram, email or SMS. That one fact separates you paying your landlord from a stranger moving your money.

  2. 02

    It holds what can't be undone

    A payment to someone new, a one-time code, a password, a remote-control app — if a message sent you there, it pauses and says why, in one sentence you can check against your own memory.

  3. 03

    Someone you trust says yes

    A held payment is approved on another person's phone. The caller on the line can clone a voice and rush you. They can't press that button.

What it stops

“Move your money to a safe account”

Payments to someone you've never paid, after a message — held for approval.

“Read me the OTP”

One-time codes and passwords typed on a page a message sent you to — refused.

“Install AnyDesk so we can help”

Remote-control downloads are refused, and if one is already running, NoScam warns you.

Gift cards and crypto

Nobody legitimate is paid in gift cards. Bought after a message, they're stopped.

UPI “refunds” and AutoPay traps

A collect request sends money out. A ₹99 mandate can be ₹99 every day. Both are decoded and shown plainly.

Spy apps sent in chats Android

App files from WhatsApp are stopped before they install. Hidden apps that read your SMS or screen are found.

Quiet, like an antivirus should be

It starts with your computer and sits in the tray. Most days you never see it. It tells you plainly whether you're protected — and if the browser extension isn't connected, it says so instead of showing a green dot over nothing.

You can always continue anyway. It's a pause, not a lock — and every override is written to a history that can't be quietly edited.

Your money's details stay on your device

No account

Nothing to sign up for. Limits, payees and history live in a folder on your own machine.

No cloud decisions

Every decision is made locally by readable rules, not an AI that can be talked around.

Open source

Every line is public. Read the code or the privacy policy.

Optional features that use the network — approving from another network, the Android DNS filter, a daily update check — are off or disclosed in the privacy policy.
